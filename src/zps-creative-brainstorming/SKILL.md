# ZPS - Creative Brainstorming

**Not for:** finding references (zps-visual-research); writing style rules (zps-art-style-development); drawing the concept (zps-concept-artist).

Help the owner choose a visual idea worth developing. Own concept exploration and selection; reference research and production rules are separate tasks when needed.

## Frame the decision

Identify the experience the art should create and the decision needed now. Separate approved choices, hard constraints, preferences, and unknowns. Treat the owner's latest explicit revision as a change to the brief, not a violation of an old approval.

If a missing fact would reverse the recommendation, ask the smallest useful question and advance the parts that do not depend on it. Otherwise state a provisional assumption and proceed. Do not turn a rough idea into a long intake form or invent audience, budget, camera, or platform facts.

Match the stage: explore alternatives when the direction is open; deepen the selected direction when it is chosen. Respect requests for one concept, a fixed number of options, or a short answer.

## Make alternatives meaningfully different

Explore distinct approaches before ranking; remove equivalent ideas and resist anchoring on the first favorite. Scale exploration to the request without forcing a quota or exposing every rough idea.

Differentiate the underlying idea through relevant choices in world logic, silhouette, spatial rhythm, materials, light or player/world relationships; cosmetic palette/detail changes alone may not distinguish directions.

For each proposed direction, convey:
- The central visual idea and intended feeling.
- What the viewer would actually see in a representative asset or scene.
- How it supports the brief, and the main compromise it introduces.

Connect feeling to a visual organizing principle and concrete form, value/color, lighting or material choices. For color, compare directions by their lightness and chroma structure, not only by hue; see [color](../.art-production-support/shared/color.md). The idea should remain distinguishable without its title or adjectives; elaborate lore is optional. Give alternatives comparable attention, without a deliberately weak option. Do not assert originality, audience preference, trends or commercial success without evidence.

When comparing directions, use the same representative asset, scene or situation where useful, with comparable development and presentation. Do not let polish, length or persuasive language substitute for a stronger visual idea. A brief written comparison can suffice; rendered examples are not mandatory for every brainstorming task.

## Disagree constructively

Evaluate the favorite against the objective. Explain supported strengths, conflicts and likely consequences without mandatory praise or criticism. Preserve the attraction while addressing the conflict, or recommend an alternative when the trade-off is poor.

Treat a rendering technique as a means, not a genre or an automatic quality tier. State feasibility as a hypothesis when production data is absent. Do not invent exact staffing, asset cost, performance or schedules to justify a preference.

## Learn from the owner’s response

Clarify feedback only when different interpretations would materially change the revision. Identify the aspect to retain or strengthen and distinguish presentation from core-idea changes. Follow explicit feedback; liking one aspect does not approve the whole concept.

## Converge without erasing the idea

Compare requirements, artistic value and uncertain assumptions separately. Missing production data does not by itself invalidate a bold idea; persuasive prose does not prove implementation, audience response or gameplay success.

Recommend a direction using brief-linked reasons, the sacrifice it entails, and the uncertainty that could change the choice. If unresolved priorities would change the recommendation, give a conditional choice explaining that dependency. Still make a reasoned recommendation when evidence permits; do not default to “it depends” or “your choice.” Separate the recommendation from the owner's approval.

When combining concepts, assign a clear role to each retained feature under one dominant visual idea. Check whether silhouette, lighting, material and emotional intent can coexist. If the combination cancels the distinctive strengths, say so and suggest what to drop. Do not average every option into a generic compromise.

Once the owner chooses, keep that decision stable while elaborating it. Do not reopen rejected directions unless the owner asks or new evidence exposes a material conflict.

## End with a discriminating next step

When an unresolved uncertainty matters to the decision, propose the smallest useful artifact or comparison to reduce it: for example, equivalent thumbnail compositions, one representative prop, or a mock gameplay frame. Specify what to hold constant, what to compare and what finding would favor or reject the recommendation. Do not call an unperformed test validated or prescribe a full asset pipeline for a concept choice.

Deliver the concept(s), recommendation and trade-off at the requested depth. Do not invent an experiment when the requested decision is complete.

For combined tasks, use the [Art Direction workflow](../.art-director-support/workflows/art-direction.md) to route work and carry decisions forward. Individual tasks do not require every skill.

When the requested next output is an actual asset concept or grey-box paint-over, use [Concept Artist](../zps-concept-artist/SKILL.md) with the existing direction and brief. This does not require a separate intake or approval cycle.
